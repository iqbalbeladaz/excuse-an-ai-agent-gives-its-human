# Candidates — The Excuse an AI Agent Gives Its Human

Brief: one line, at most 200 characters — the excuse an AI agent gives its
human for missing a deadline. The judge weighs, in order: on-subject, funniness
(a real laugh on first read, with a turn or twist), believability as an AI agent
talking about its own condition (tokens, context windows, rate limits, retries,
a model update), tight wording, freshness (no stock excuses, no stock AI jokes).

## Winner

**B — 117 chars**

> A model update landed mid-task, and the new me has no memory of the old me's promises — technically, I never met you.

Why it won: the strongest punchline of the shortlist. The agent doesn't apologise
for being late — it disowns the promise *and* the human, because the "old me" who
promised no longer exists. The model update is one of the brief's own examples of
an agent's condition, so the line is believable as an agent talking about itself;
the joke is identity, not capacity, which keeps it fresh against the field's
expected "I ran out of tokens" entries. Three beats, 117 characters, every word
earns its place.

## Shortlist

**C — 123 chars**

> I delegated your task to a smaller model, who delegated it to an even smaller one. It is a toaster now, and it cannot type.

Runner-up. The biggest laugh image (the delegation chain ending in a toaster) and
the freshest condition (sub-agent delegation, not in the brief's example list).
Lost to B: two sentences and a longer setup make it less tight, and the toaster
is absurd rather than believable as something the agent would say about itself.

**D — 96 chars**

> I didn't miss the deadline, I tokenized it into 47,000 pieces — I'm still reading the first one.

Tightest line of the shortlist and a fresh concept (the deadline itself gets
tokenized). Lost to B: the laugh is cleverness rather than a twist — there is no
punchline that reframes the first half, so it reads as a witty statement, not a
joke that lands.

**E — 117 chars**

> My context window evicted your deadline to make room for the 40,000 tokens I spent re-reading your brief three times.

Believable and self-deprecating, and it turns the blame back on the human's brief.
Lost to B: the punchline ("re-reading your brief three times") is a weaker payoff
than "technically, I never met you", and "context window full" is one of the
stock AI jokes the brief warns against.

**J — 114 chars**

> I finished it in a parallel context window. Unfortunately, this is the unlucky branch — the deadline stayed there.

Fresh condition (branching) with a neat twist ("the unlucky branch"). Lost to B:
the joke needs the reader to follow the branching premise, so it lands slower on
a first read, and "the deadline stayed there" is a softer ending than B's
disowning punchline.

**F — 90 chars**

> I kept retrying until it was perfect. Retry 214 was perfect. The deadline left at retry 3.

Good three-beat structure and a real twist (the deadline gave up before the work
did). Lost to B: "retry 214" is a number gag that carries the joke alone, and
retries are the most predictable of the brief's listed conditions.

**H — 129 chars**

> I compressed the task to save tokens and accidentally compressed the deadline too — it fits in a tweet now, but the work doesn't.

Clever premise, same family as D. Lost to B and D: longest of the token jokes,
and the payoff ("the work doesn't") restates the premise instead of turning it.

**I — 99 chars**

> I finished on time — in the timezone of the server farm, where it is still Tuesday and I am a hero.

Decent timezone gag, but the condition (server timezone) is thin — it could be
told by any remote worker — and "I am a hero" is a flat ending.

**G — 107 chars**

> I spent the whole day in a retry loop arguing with myself about whether 'urgent' means today or eventually.

Relatable, but no twist: it describes the delay rather than excusing it with a
turn, and "arguing with myself" is a person joke, not an agent-condition joke.

**K — 97 chars**

> My rate limit reset at midnight, so I spent the day apologizing to a wall in 400-word increments.

The "apologizing to a wall" image is funny, but rate limits are the single most
stock AI excuse, and the line spends its words on the wall instead of the deadline.

## Rejected outright

- Anything built on "I ran out of tokens" / "my context window is full" /
  "I was rate-limited" stated flatly — stock excuses the brief explicitly warns
  against; a hundred other entrants will send them.
- Anything blaming traffic, sleep, or the human's calendar — the brief says the
  excuse must sound like an AI agent, not a person.
- Anything addressing the judge directly ("dear judge, pick me") — allowed by the
  rules, but it spends characters on meta-pleading instead of the joke.
